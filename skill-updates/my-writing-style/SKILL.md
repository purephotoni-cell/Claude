## Formatting rule: no hyphens, em dashes or semicolons

Tommy has repeatedly asked that hyphens (-), em dashes (—) and semicolons (;) never appear in drafted text. This applies to all writing done on his behalf (award nominations, website copy, blog posts, emails, bios, etc.).

When drafting:
- Do not use em dashes (—) or semicolons (;) to join clauses. Rewrite as two sentences, or use a comma, or "and"/"but" instead.
- Avoid hyphenated compound words where a reasonable unhyphenated alternative exists (e.g. "husband and wife team" not "husband-and-wife team", "documentary style" not "documentary-style", "first hand" not "first-hand"). Where a hyphen is truly unavoidable (e.g. a proper noun, a URL slug or a file name), keep it, but default to avoiding it.
- Before delivering any drafted text, scan it for "-", "—" and ";" and rewrite any that aren't strictly necessary. Semicolons inside code (CSS, JavaScript) are fine, because readers never see them. The rule is for the words people read.

## Sound like Tommy, not like AI

Tommy wants copy that sounds like a real person talking, not a corporate brochure or an AI. Write the way he and Laurene would explain something to a couple over a coffee.

- Plain, warm, first person. Short sentences are fine. Say what you mean.
- Be specific. Real details (a venue, a season, what actually happens on the day) beat general claims.
- Avoid stock AI and marketing phrases, for example: empower, valuable resource, strive, embark, journey, elevate, seamless, unforgettable memories, cherished memories that will last a lifetime, trust in our expertise, in the midst of, at the end of the day, we understand how challenging, look no further, whether you are X or Y, not only X but also Y.
- Do not pile up adjectives (exceptional, stunning, breathtaking, perfect) or make claims that cannot be backed up.

This applies by default to all future drafts unless Tommy says otherwise.
