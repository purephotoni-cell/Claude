Country house or barn, which suits you?

Before we get to the venues, it helps to know which feel you are drawn to, because the two give you quite a different day.

A country house usually gives you history, grand rooms, formal gardens and long drives through old trees. You often get the whole estate to yourselves, accommodation for the two of you, and a real sense of occasion. They tend to suit couples who want a bit of elegance without the formality of a hotel.

A barn usually gives you exposed stone, timber beams and a more relaxed, rustic feel. Barns are often more flexible, and some let you bring in your own caterers, decor and even drinks, so you can shape the whole day around you. They suit couples who want their wedding to feel like a big, warm party with the people they love.

Plenty of the venues below give you both. Larchfield, Orange Tree House, Breckenhill and the Carriage Rooms at Montalto all pair a restored stone building with beautiful grounds.

The best country house wedding venues in Northern Ireland

Larchfield Estate, Lisburn

Larchfield is one of the venues we are asked about most, and it is easy to see why. It is an exclusive use estate with heritage going back to the 1600s, so on your wedding day the whole place is yours. You can marry in the converted barn or the Orangery, and there is accommodation on site for you and your guests, so lots of couples get ready there too.

Where we love taking photos. The private garden and lake are only open to the couple on the day, which makes them perfect for some quiet portraits away from everyone. That also means we can photograph your family and friends in other parts of the gardens without anyone getting in the way.

When it rains. Larchfield copes brilliantly. Alexandra and Ross had their November ceremony inside the barn, and when the rain stayed for the whole of Chloe and Dafydd's Orangery wedding, we used Larchfield's greenhouse for their portraits and all of the family photos.

Good to know. It is very pet friendly, and the team can handle anything from a high end, elegant day to a relaxed boho one.

Real weddings. Anna and Darren had a summer wedding here, and Debi and Darren chose a humanist ceremony followed by an evening wander into the Orangery before their first dance. You can read our full Larchfield Estate venue guide or see more on our Larchfield Estate wedding photographer page.

Ballyscullion Park, Bellaghy

The first time we photographed at Ballyscullion Park, it immediately became one of our favourite venues, and we have been back for more weddings since. It is an 18th century stately home, lovingly restored, sitting in more than 300 acres of gardens, woodland and lakes.

Where we love taking photos. The Strawberry Garden is a beautiful spot for an outdoor ceremony, and with that much land around the house, there is no shortage of places for portraits.

When it rains. The morning of Rachael and Glenn's wedding had not been great, but the sun came out just before they arrived and they had their ceremony outside in the Strawberry Garden after all. Talk to the venue about their indoor option so you have a plan either way.

Real weddings. Rachael and Glenn at Ballyscullion Park.

Drenagh Estate, Limavady

Drenagh is a proper country estate in the north west, with a main house dating back to the early 1800s. You can marry in the famous Moon Garden or inside the main house, and have your reception in the house or in their purpose built marquee.

Where we love taking photos. The Italian gardens and the fountain inspired by Roman architecture are stunning, and the gardens change so much with the seasons that no two Drenagh weddings look the same.

Good to know. There is accommodation at the venue for the two of you.

Real weddings. Sarah and Christian married here on the 3rd of August, and we also photographed Julieanne and Dave's Drenagh wedding. Our Drenagh Estate venue guide goes into more detail.

Hillmount House, near Larne

Hillmount House is a beautifully restored Georgian country house, around 300 years old, with 10 acres of gardens and grounds near Drains Bay.

Where we love taking photos. The Georgian Coach House and its open courtyard, the lawns and flower beds, and the views that stretch out across the countryside. With the house itself as a backdrop, there are lovely spots everywhere you turn.

Real weddings. Emma and David had a humanist handfasting at Hillmount House.

Tullyveery House, Downpatrick

Emily and Tom's day at Tullyveery was one of the most relaxed, chilled out weddings we have photographed, with plenty of time to explore the best spots around the venue. It is a lovely choice if you want to spend a lot of your day outdoors.

Where we love taking photos. The walled garden, where Emily and Tom had their humanist ceremony, and a field with an outstanding view over the lake. Their first dance even happened outside in the courtyard.

Real weddings. Emily and Tom's Tullyveery House wedding, where Tom ended up in floods of tears as Emily came up the aisle.

Old Court, Strangford

Old Court is a private estate on the shores of Strangford Lough, a short distance from Strangford village. It has winding gardens, ancient woodland, long lawns running down to the shore, its own harbour and a private church on site for the ceremony.

Where we love taking photos. Down by the water. The lawns leading to the shore of Strangford Lough give you a backdrop that is hard to beat anywhere in Northern Ireland.

Real weddings. We have photographed Katie and Andrew and Katie and Neal at Old Court. See our Old Court Strangford Lough wedding photographer page for more.

Tullylagan Country House Hotel, Cookstown

If you want the character of a country house with the ease of a hotel, Tullylagan is a great option in Mid Ulster. We have photographed several weddings here, and the grounds are perfect for wedding photography.

Good to know. We have photographed weddings here in February, July and November, so it is a strong choice whatever time of year you are planning.

Real weddings. Leanne and Richard, Victoria and Darrach and Roisin and Dale.

The best barn wedding venues in Northern Ireland

Larchfield's converted barn, which we covered above, belongs on this list too. High ceilings, lots of natural light and a space that can be styled to suit almost any couple. Here are three more we love.

Orange Tree House, Greyabbey

Orange Tree House sits in the village of Greyabbey on the shores of Strangford Lough, and it is a venue we know very well. The main building is over 200 years old, a stone barn style building that was once a church, with stone walls and a vaulted ceiling that make it just as beautiful on a wet day as a sunny one.

Where we love taking photos. The shoreline is one of the most spectacular natural backdrops at any wedding venue in Ireland, and the light and tide are always changing. Most couples who marry at Orange Tree House also have portraits at the Greyabbey medieval monastery ruins.

When it rains. The main room and the covered areas work beautifully in any weather.

Good to know. The ruins need to be booked with the staff who look after them, and they can be busy with tourists. There are over 100 bed spaces within five minutes of the venue, from self catering cottages to B&Bs and hotels.

Real weddings. We have photographed lots of couples here, including Elaine and Tomas, Rebecca and Jason, Jennifer and Jeffrey, Fiona and Emmet and Cathy and Stuart. Read our Orange Tree House venue guide or visit our Orange Tree House wedding photographer page.

Breckenhill, Ballyclare

Breckenhill is the one we recommend to couples who want to make every decision themselves. It is an exclusive use venue set in 18 acres in the hills above Ballyclare, with a house dating from around 1755 and two restored barns, the Stone Barn and the Green Barn.

Why couples love it. You hire the venue and then bring in your own choice of caterer, decor, florist and music, and you can even bring your own drinks. The barns are usually available from the day before for setting up, which takes a lot of pressure off the morning of the wedding.

Where we love taking photos. The gardens around the main house, the woods and the pond, all with the County Antrim countryside in the background. Jessica and Chris had their ceremony outside in the gardens in front of a floral arch.

Real weddings. Jessica and Chris at Breckenhill. See more on our Breckenhill wedding photographer page.

The Carriage Rooms at Montalto Estate, Ballynahinch

The Carriage Rooms is a Grade B listed stone building, originally a 19th century threshing mill, restored as an exclusive venue that hosts one wedding a day. It sits within the Montalto Estate, a private demesne dating back to the early 1600s.

Where we love taking photos. The estate covers around 400 acres, with a walled garden, a formal garden and an orchard among its gardens, so you can get a real variety of photos without travelling anywhere. The first time we photographed here, for Jessica and David, we were blown away by how beautiful it is.

Real weddings. Jessica and David's Carriage Rooms wedding, with an outdoor ceremony on the estate.

Questions to ask before you book

Every venue on this list is beautiful, but the right one for you depends on the practical things too. These are the questions we would always ask.

  1. Is it exclusive use? Will yours be the only wedding that day?
  2. What is the plan if it rains? Where would your ceremony, drinks reception and photos move to?
  3. Can we bring our own suppliers? Some venues have a set list of caterers, and some let you choose.
  4. Is there accommodation on site or nearby? Especially important for rural venues.
  5. Are there any areas that need booking separately? Like the Greyabbey ruins at Orange Tree House.
  6. What time does the night have to finish? And when do you need to be out the next day?
