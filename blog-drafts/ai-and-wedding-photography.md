A couple of years ago nobody asked us about AI. Now it comes up at almost every wedding consultation, usually with a slightly nervous laugh. Couples have seen the stories online about galleries full of strange looking faces, skies that look like stock photos and moments that never actually happened. So we wanted to put it all in one place and be completely open with you about where we stand. The short version is simple: every photograph we give you is a real moment that happened in front of our camera on your wedding day.

## What AI can do to wedding photos in 2026

It is worth being honest about how far this technology has come, because it is genuinely impressive. Phones can now go through a burst of group photos and swap in each person's best expression from a different frame. Apps can change a face, open closed eyes, add a sunset that was never there or remove a guest from a shot entirely. Some services will even build a full set of "wedding photos" from a couple of selfies, without anyone ever holding a camera.

There have been some well publicised horror stories too. Couples have received galleries where the faces of their family just did not look like their family, or where a photographer used AI to "fix" images and the originals were lost. Wedding magazines have started refusing to publish AI imagery, and the wider wedding industry is having a real debate about where the line should be.

We think that line is very easy to see once you ask one question: did this moment actually happen?

## Why real wedding photography matters more than ever

Your wedding photos are not just nice pictures. They are a record. Years from now, your children and grandchildren will look at them to see who was there, how your Granny laughed during the speeches and what your Dad's face looked like when he saw you for the first time. If any of that has been invented by a computer, the photo stops being a memory and becomes a guess.

That is why we will never use AI to create, add or change a moment in your wedding photos. That means:

* We will never generate a photo of something that did not happen.
* We will never swap faces or expressions between different images, even in the group photos.
* We will never add people, skies, sunsets or scenery that were not there.
* We will never change how you or your guests actually look.

What we do is the same thing good wedding photographers have always done. We adjust colour, exposure and contrast so your images look their best, the same way film photographers did in the darkroom, and we might tidy up small distractions like a fire exit sign in the background of a portrait. The people, the expressions and the moments are always exactly as they were.

If someone blinked in a group photo, the answer is not to paste in a face from somewhere else. The answer is to take a few extra frames, which is exactly what we do. You can read more about how we approach this in our guide to [family and bridal party group photos at weddings](https://www.purephotoni.com/blog/group-photos-at-weddings).

## Real moments are the whole point

Our style has always been documentary. We are not there to create a perfect, polished version of your day. We are there to catch the real one: the happy tears, the nervous laughter before the ceremony, the moment the dance floor fills up. The little imperfections are often what make a photo special. A windswept veil on the north coast or a slightly blurry flower girl running across the lawn tells the true story of your day far better than anything a computer could make up.

It is also why we care so much about helping you feel relaxed in front of the camera. If you are worried about how you will look, our post on [feeling comfortable in front of the camera on your wedding day](https://www.purephotoni.com/blog/how-to-feel-comfortable-in-front-of-the-camera-on-your-wedding-day) is a good place to start. When couples feel at ease, the real moments take care of themselves, and there is never any need to fake anything.

## Questions to ask any wedding photographer about AI

Whoever you book, it is worth asking a few simple questions before you sign anything:

1. Do you ever use AI to generate or add anything to the images you deliver?
2. Do you swap faces or expressions between photos?
3. What editing do you do, and will my photos still look like the real day?
4. Do you keep the original files safely?

A good photographer will be happy to answer all of these clearly. It is also worth checking what your contract says about [image usage rights and copyright](https://www.purephotoni.com/blog/wedding-photography-image-usage-rights-and-copyright), so you know exactly how your photos can be used.
