On the 29th of December 2022 we photographed Claire and Chris at The Wool Tower in Broughshane. It was bitterly cold, it rained on and off all day, and their first dance lasted about 20 seconds before their friends piled onto the floor to join them. If we ran those photos through some of the AI tools around today, we could give them blue skies, a slow dance that never happened and a room with fewer umbrellas in it. It would look lovely, and it would not be their wedding. That is the problem with AI in wedding photography in one sentence, and it is the reason we wanted to write this post. Some of our couples have asked us where we stand on AI, so here is our answer, with no fudging.

## What AI is already doing to wedding photos

A lot of this has crept in quietly, so it is worth spelling out what is already possible.

**Your phone is already doing it.** Back in 2023 Google added a feature called Best Take to its Pixel phones. It looks through a burst of group photos, finds everyone's face, and lets you swap in each person's best expression from a different frame. So Auntie's smile might come from one shot and the best man's open eyes from another. The result is a photo of a moment that never happened, and most people using it never think twice about that.

**Apps will make you a whole wedding.** There are now apps that will take a couple of selfies and generate a full set of "wedding photos", complete with a dress you never wore at a venue you never visited. Others offer face swaps across whole bridal party shots so that nobody is ever caught blinking.

**Well meaning "fixes" can go badly wrong.** One story that did the rounds on Reddit's r/weddingshaming forum really stuck with us. A groom's uncle, a former sports photographer, took the photos at a family wedding. He felt the lighting was poor, so he ran the images through an AI editor. The faces came back unrecognisable, and in some photos the groom's face was erased entirely. Worst of all, he had saved over the originals. The couple ended up asking guests for their phone snaps just to fill an album.

**The wedding industry is pushing back.** In April 2026 Love My Dress, one of the biggest wedding publications in the UK, changed its submission guidelines to say it will not knowingly publish AI generated imagery. Its founder, Annabel Beeforth, followed that up in September with a longer piece on what AI imagery is costing photographers, makers and artists. When a magazine built entirely on beautiful pictures says it only wants real ones, that tells you something.

## Where we draw the line

We are not going to pretend that no software touches your photos. Every digital photo is edited in some way, and it always has been. Film photographers dodged and burned in the darkroom for the same reasons we adjust images on a screen. So it is more useful to be specific about what we do and what we never do.

**What we do.** We adjust colour, exposure, white balance and contrast so that skin looks like skin and a grey Northern Ireland afternoon still looks like the day you remember. We will tidy up small distractions, like a green fire exit sign glowing over your shoulder in a portrait or a stray hair tie on a wrist.

To be completely open, the only AI we use in our editing is what is built into Lightroom and Photoshop, such as the content aware tools, and we only use it for exactly those small fixes. It removes a spot or a sign. It never adds anything to your day or changes a moment. Our favourite images often end up in black and white too, which we wrote about in [why black and white wedding photography is so emotional](https://www.purephotoni.com/blog/why-black-and-white-wedding-photography-is-timeless-and-emotional).

**What we will never do.**

* Generate any part of a photo that was not in front of our cameras.
* Swap faces or expressions between frames, even in the family group photos.
* Change the shape of anyone's face or body.
* Add skies, sunsets, scenery or people that were not there.
* Deliver a single image that shows something which did not happen.

The test we use is simple. If your grandchildren pick up your album in 40 years and point at a photo, could you say "that is exactly how it was"? If the answer is no, it does not go in your gallery.

So what happens when someone blinks in the group photos? The same thing that has always happened. We take a few extra frames of every group, keep an eye on the back row and call out anyone who has wandered off to the bar. Our guide to [family and bridal party group photos](https://www.purephotoni.com/blog/group-photos-at-weddings) explains how we run that part of the day so it is quick and nobody gets missed.

## The moments a computer could never make up

Since 2014 the two of us have photographed hundreds of weddings across Northern Ireland, and when we look back, the images couples love most are rarely the most polished ones. They are the things nobody could have planned.

At Bethany and Andrew's wedding at the [Burrendale Hotel](https://www.purephotoni.com/blog/burrendale-hotel-wedding) in Newcastle, a little model cottage sat on display. It was the last thing Bethany's grandfather made before he passed away, and it was there so he was part of the day. No AI tool would know to include it, or why it mattered so much to that family.

At Nina and Jonny's [La Mon Hotel wedding](https://www.purephotoni.com/blog/la-mon-hotel-belfast-wedding-photographer), there was a choreographed father and daughter dance and a Dachshund called Toby who had his own fan club by the end of the night. You cannot prompt your way to Toby.

Then there was Claire and Chris, with their rain and their 20 second first dance. That wedding is more memorable because of the weather, not in spite of it. The real day has texture that a generated one never will. Think of the wind catching a veil at Dunluce, a flower girl running off mid ceremony, a Dad who swore he would not cry and then did.

That is why we care so much about helping couples relax in front of the camera rather than fixing things afterwards. ## Why your original files matter more than ever

Back in 2014 we wrote a post called [The Most Captured Generation in History](https://www.purephotoni.com/blog/2014/10/13/the-most-photographed-generation-ever). The worry then was that people were taking more photos than ever but might end up with none, because we had gone from CD to DVD to USB stick to online galleries in only a few years and older formats were already dying off.

That risk has not gone away, but AI has added a new one. It is no longer only about whether your photos survive. It is whether you can trust that they are real. The uncle on Reddit did not only ruin the images, he overwrote the originals, so there was no way back. The originals are the proof of what actually happened on your day, so it is worth asking any photographer how they look after them.

Our own advice is simple. As soon as your gallery arrives, download the full set and keep copies in at least two places, like a computer and a cloud storage account or an external hard drive. Please do not rely on any photographer, us included, to be the only place your wedding photos live. And if you would like prints or an album, our post on [whether you should buy a wedding album](https://www.purephotoni.com/blog/should-you-buy-a-wedding-album) explains why a physical copy is still one of the safest backups there is.

There is also some good news on the technology side. Camera makers have started building "Content Credentials" into their cameras. This is an open standard, known as C2PA, backed by Adobe, Google, Sony, Nikon, Canon, Leica, the BBC and others. It attaches a secure record to a photo showing where it came from and what has been done to it since. The Leica M11-P was the first camera to include it in 2023, Sony has added it to some of its Alpha cameras, and Canon launched its own system in May 2026. It is aimed at news photographers for now, but it is easy to imagine a future where couples can check that their wedding photos are the real thing.

## Questions to ask any wedding photographer about AI

Whoever you book, these are fair questions to ask before you sign a contract. Any good photographer will be happy to answer them plainly.

1. **Do you use AI to generate or add anything to the photos you deliver?** You want a clear no.
2. **Do you swap faces or expressions between frames?** Group photos are where this is most tempting, so ask about them specifically.
3. **What does your editing actually involve?** Look at full galleries, not only highlights on Instagram, and ask whether they look like real days.
4. **How long do you keep the original files?** And do they keep them separately from the edited versions?
5. **What does the contract say about how my images can be used?** Our post on [image usage rights and copyright](https://www.purephotoni.com/blog/wedding-photography-image-usage-rights-and-copyright) covers what to look for.
