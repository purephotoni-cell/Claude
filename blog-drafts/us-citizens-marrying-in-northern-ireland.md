## Can US citizens legally get married in Northern Ireland?

Yes. There is no residency requirement, so you do not need to live here or arrive weeks in advance. You can have a legal civil ceremony with a registrar, a religious ceremony, or a humanist ceremony, and all of them follow the same legal steps. Same sex marriage has been legal in Northern Ireland since January 2020.

Your marriage will be registered in the UK, and marriages that are legally valid where they take place are generally recognised in the United States. You do not need to register it with the US Embassy afterwards.

## The mistake most US couples make with their visa

This is the single most important part of this guide, so please read it even if you skip everything else.

As a US citizen, you do not need a visa to visit the UK as a tourist. Since January 2025 you do need an Electronic Travel Authorisation, or ETA, which you apply for online. Lots of couples assume that is all they need for their wedding too.

It is not. The UK government is very clear that you cannot give notice of marriage or get married with an ETA. If you are coming to Northern Ireland to legally marry, you need a **Marriage Visitor visa**, and the registrar needs to see it when you give notice.

If you give notice without the right visa, you will be referred to the Home Office for investigation and your notice period can be extended from 28 days to 70 days. That can be enough to push your wedding past the date you have booked everything for.

A few key facts about the Marriage Visitor visa as of 2026.

* You apply online from the USA before you travel.
* You can apply up to 3 months before your travel date.
* The standard decision time is around 3 weeks.
* The fee rose to £135 in April 2026.
* It lets you stay for up to 6 months, and your wedding has to happen within that time.
* You will need to show evidence of your wedding plans and that you intend to leave the UK afterwards.

**A quick note if one of you is a British or Irish citizen.** This guide is for two US citizens visiting to marry and then going home. If you are marrying a British or Irish citizen and planning to live here afterwards, the visa route is different, and it is worth speaking to an immigration adviser before you book anything.

## How to legally get married in Northern Ireland, step by step

**Step 1. Choose your date, ceremony type and location.** Book your registrar, minister or humanist celebrant first, because the legal paperwork is tied to the council district where your ceremony will take place. If you are having a humanist ceremony, our guide on [how to choose a wedding celebrant in Northern Ireland](https://www.purephotoni.com/blog/how-do-i-choose-a-wedding-celebrant-in-northern-ireland) is a good place to start.

**Step 2. Gather your documents.** More on exactly what you need below. Order US documents early, because some states take weeks to send certified copies.

**Step 3. Apply for your Marriage Visitor visa.** Up to 3 months before you travel. You need the visa before you give notice.

**Step 4. Give notice to the registrar.** Each of you fills in your own marriage notice form, called the GRO 446. It goes to the registration office for the council district where you are marrying. You can post it from the USA, and some councils accept email, so check with yours. The legal minimum is 28 days before the wedding, and you can give notice up to 12 months in advance. The official advice is to allow around 8 weeks, or 10 weeks if either of you has been married before.

**Step 5. Include your immigration status statement.** Because you are not UK, Irish, EEA or Swiss nationals, you each fill in an immigration status statement and send it with your notice, along with your passport details and visa.

**Step 6. Collect your marriage schedule.** The marriage schedule is the legal document that gets signed on the day. One of you has to collect it in person from the registration office where you gave notice, and it cannot be collected any earlier than 14 days before the wedding. So plan to be in Northern Ireland at least a few days before collection opens, not flying in the night before.

**Step 7. Get married.** You need two witnesses aged 16 or over. If you are eloping and have not brought anyone with you, that is not a problem. We are always happy to act as your witnesses, and your celebrant can usually help too.

**Step 8. Return the schedule within 3 working days.** Once it is signed, the schedule has to go back to the same registration office within 3 working days, either in person or by post. After that your marriage is registered and you can order your certificate.

## A realistic timeline, working back from your wedding day

Here is how we would plan it if you were getting married on a Saturday in the middle of June.

* **12 to 18 months before.** Book your ceremony, your celebrant or registrar, and your photographer. Popular summer Saturdays go early.
* **6 months before.** Order certified copies of your birth certificates, and any divorce or death certificates, from the relevant US state offices.
* **Around 3 months before you travel.** Apply for your Marriage Visitor visa.
* **8 to 10 weeks before the wedding.** Once your visa is granted, post your GRO 446 forms and immigration status statements to the registrar. Leave extra time for postage in both directions.
* **About 2 weeks before.** Fly in, find your feet and get over the jet lag.
* **14 days before or less.** Collect your marriage schedule in person.
* **Your wedding day.** Sign the schedule with your two witnesses.
* **Within 3 working days.** Return the signed schedule.
* **Before you fly home.** Order your marriage certificate, and ideally get it apostilled.

The squeeze in the middle is the part to watch. You cannot apply for the visa more than 3 months before you travel, but you also need that visa before you can give notice. If you are flying in close to the wedding, that window gets tight quickly, so we would always recommend arriving earlier rather than later.

## The documents you need

You will each need

* A completed GRO 446 marriage notice form.
* An immigration status statement.
* Your passport and Marriage Visitor visa.
* Your full birth certificate. In the US this means the long form certified copy that shows your parents' details, not a short abstract or a photocopy.

If either of you has been married before, you will also need one of these.

* A certified copy of your final divorce decree, or
* The death certificate of your former spouse.

The registrar needs to see original documents. For couples living overseas, registrars generally accept the original notice forms and birth certificates by post with photocopies of your passports, but every council handles this a little differently, so ask yours exactly what they want before you post anything. Always use tracked post, and keep copies of everything.

**If you have been divorced in the US,** give yourself the longer 10 week notice period. The registrar has to be satisfied that you are free to marry, and checking a foreign divorce can take extra time.

## What it costs

These are the legal and admin costs we know about as of 2026. Ceremony fees vary by council and by ceremony type, so check the current figure with your registrar or celebrant.

* **Marriage Visitor visa** £135 each.
* **Marriage notice fee** £22 each, so £44 for the two of you.
* **Marriage certificate** a small fee when you order it.
* **Apostille** £45 per certificate from the UK government for a paper apostille.
* **Ceremony fee** depends on your council, venue and celebrant.

## If you would rather marry legally at home first

Plenty of US couples do the legal part at a courthouse back home and then have a symbolic ceremony here. It is a completely valid choice, and it has some real advantages.

* You do not need the Marriage Visitor visa. You would visit as a tourist with an ETA, which costs £20 and lasts 2 years.
* There is no notice period or marriage schedule to worry about.
* Your celebrant can hold a ceremony that feels every bit as meaningful, with your vows, readings, a handfasting or anything else you want to include. Our post on [what a handfasting ceremony is](https://www.purephotoni.com/blog/what-is-a-handfasting-ceremony) has a few ideas.

The trade off is that your wedding date on paper will be a different day to the one you celebrate. For some couples that matters a lot, and for others it does not matter at all. There is no wrong answer.

## Northern Ireland or the Republic of Ireland?

We get asked this a lot, especially by couples who have been reading guides that treat the whole island as one place. They are two different countries with two different legal systems. For a legal wedding, Northern Ireland is often the simpler option for couples coming from the USA.

* **Notice period.** In Northern Ireland the minimum is 28 days. In the Republic of Ireland it is 3 months.
* **Giving notice from abroad.** In Northern Ireland you can post your forms. In the Republic of Ireland you can apply for permission to notify by post, but you both still have to meet the registrar in person at least 5 days before the wedding.
* **Fees.** Notice in Northern Ireland costs £22 each. In the Republic the notification fee is €200.
* **Visas.** Northern Ireland is part of the UK, so UK rules apply, including the Marriage Visitor visa. The Republic has its own immigration system.

Remember that whichever side of the border you marry on, the paperwork has to be done in that jurisdiction. You cannot give notice in Belfast and get married in Donegal.
